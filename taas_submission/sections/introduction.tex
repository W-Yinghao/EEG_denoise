\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

Electroencephalography (EEG) recorded near the eyes contains activity propagated
from blinks and eye movements. The propagation depends on anatomy, electrode
placement, impedance, and reference, so a population correction can be poorly
matched to a new participant. Electrooculography (EOG) provides a direct
measurement of ocular activity, but the transfer from EOG to EEG remains
participant dependent. This motivates a narrowly scoped adaptation question:
does an early EEG+EOG calibration improve a fixed diffusion denoiser on later
EEG from the same participant?

We study this question under a disjoint early-support to later-query protocol.
The support segment estimates an EOG-to-EEG contamination operator without
using participant identity. During later inference, both EEG and EOG are
observed. The method is therefore \emph{EOG-guided}, not EEG-only. Clean targets,
motor-imagery labels, and evaluator annotations are not visible to the model.
The matched operator supplies a linear artifact anchor to a deterministic
residual network and a conditional diffusion network. Population and
mismatched-subject operators can replace the matched operator while weights,
query windows, and random streams remain fixed. This isolates the effect of the
support-derived subject context inside one frozen denoising pipeline.

The experiment uses BCI Competition IV-2b and covers all nine development
participants. An early same-session calibration segment precedes later query
windows. We report paired real-EEG/EOG-backed semi-simulation for waveform
efficacy and natural motor-imagery EEG for artifact attenuation and neural-signal
proxies. The three training seeds are stability repeats on the same cohort,
not independent scientific samples or confirmation.

The result is specific but consistent: participant-averaged RRMSE improves by
$0.03473$ relative to population conditioning and by $0.04836$ relative to a
frozen cyclic unseen-subject operator. Both effects are positive for 9/9
participants. The result does not show that diffusion is the strongest estimator:
DIFF-MATCH has RRMSE $0.11350$, versus $0.11225$ for DET-MATCH and $0.08760$
for LINEAR-MATCH. We therefore claim a matching-subject operator effect within
the fixed diffusion pipeline, rather than diffusion-specific personalization.

The contributions are:
\begin{itemize}
  \item a disjoint same-session protocol in which early EEG+EOG calibration
  conditions later EOG-guided EEG denoising without participant identifiers;
  \item integration of a support-estimated contamination operator into a fixed
  deterministic-plus-diffusion residual estimator;
  \item direct MATCH, population, frozen cyclic unseen-WRONG, and seen-donor
  sensitivity comparisons under common queries and random streams;
  \item participant-level evidence across nine development participants and
  three training seeds, with paired waveform metrics and natural motor-imagery
  preservation proxies reported separately.
\end{itemize}
