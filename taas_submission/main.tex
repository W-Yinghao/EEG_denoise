\begin{abstract}
Ocular propagation into electroencephalography (EEG) varies across people,
making a population contamination model imperfect for a new user.  We study a
narrow deployment setting in which an early same-session EEG+EOG calibration
estimates a subject-specific EOG-to-EEG operator and conditions a fixed residual
diffusion denoiser for later EEG+EOG query windows.  Participant identity,
query clean targets, labels, and outcomes are excluded from inference.  On all
nine BCI Competition IV-2b development participants, matching calibration
reduces paired reconstruction RRMSE relative to population conditioning by
$U_P=0.03473$ and relative to a frozen cyclic unseen-subject operator by
$U_W=0.04836$.  Both effects are positive for 9/9 participants after averaging
three training seeds (one-sided exact sign-flip $p=0.001953$).  Mean natural
EEG proxies show EOG attenuation of $0.1043$ and preservation of $0.7982$,
with participant-level exceptions disclosed.  The evidence is three-seed
stability on one development cohort, not confirmation.  The method is
EOG-guided rather than EEG-only, and diffusion does not outperform the matched
deterministic or linear estimators (RRMSE $0.11350$, $0.11225$, and $0.08760$,
respectively).  The supported conclusion is therefore limited to the benefit
of matching subject calibration within this fixed diffusion pipeline.
\end{abstract}

\section*{Ethics and Privacy Statement}
This study uses de-identified research EEG released by the original dataset
providers; no new human-subject data were collected. The method does not learn
participant identifiers or biometric embeddings. Early support EEG+EOG
estimates an EOG-to-EEG contamination operator. Query EOG is an explicit
deployment input, whereas query clean targets, labels, and outcomes remain
evaluator-only. EEG and EOG remain sensitive physiological data and should be
handled under the access and privacy terms of the source dataset.
