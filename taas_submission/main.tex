%% Abstract

\begin{abstract}
Ocular propagation into EEG varies across users and recording contexts, while
most learned denoisers apply a fixed population correction.  We study
query-disjoint calibration for unseen users through support-calibrated
artifact-subspace diffusion.  Support EEG/EOG determines a rank-two transfer
basis by ridge regression, thin SVD, and within-montage Procrustes alignment.
The basis structurally defines the diffusion coordinates, observation at every
reverse step, and final EEG correction; participant identity and query EOG,
labels, or outcomes are never model inputs.  Diffusion predicts bounded artifact
coefficients and is compared with an information-matched one-step estimator
trained on the identical target.  On 16 paired real-EEG/EOG-backed Klados source
records, diffusion improves clean-RRMSE utility over the one-step estimator by
0.0592 (95\% paired-bootstrap interval [0.0336, 0.0879]) and provides better
uncertainty--error ranking.  On 58 compatible SGEYESUB participant stems, the
method satisfies frozen natural-EEG preservation margins.  However, matching
support does not improve the independent EOG endpoint over a population
operator ($-0.00515$, $[-0.0113,0.00071]$) or a same-cell wrong operator
($0.00286$, $[-0.00303,0.00867]$).  Thus iterative artifact-subspace diffusion
has development-stage value under the tested target, while a specifically
subject-aware calibration increment is not established.
\end{abstract}

\section*{Ethics and Privacy Statement}
This study uses de-identified research EEG released by the original dataset
providers; no new human-subject data were collected.  The method does not learn
participant identifiers or biometric embeddings.  A target user's short support
recording is used only to estimate an artifact propagation basis, and query EOG,
eye tracking, labels, and outcomes are excluded from inference.  EEG remains
sensitive physiological data and should be handled under the access and privacy
terms of each source dataset.
